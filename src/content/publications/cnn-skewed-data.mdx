## The question

When a dataset has far more healthy scans than Alzheimer's-positive ones, how much of a reported classifier's accuracy is actually real signal versus an artifact of the imbalance?

## What I did

Compared several CNN architectures for Alzheimer's diagnosis under class-imbalance-aware training strategies, evaluating them on metrics that don't get inflated by a skewed class distribution.

## Result

Rankings between architectures shifted once imbalance was properly accounted for — a model that looked best on raw accuracy wasn't always the best on balanced metrics.

## What it means

Any Alzheimer's classification result should be read alongside its class distribution and evaluation metric, not accuracy alone — this is a common, avoidable way results get overstated in the literature.

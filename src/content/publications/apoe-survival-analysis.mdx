## The question

Does having more copies of the APOE ε4 gene variant predict *how soon* someone with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) converts to Alzheimer's disease dementia — not just *whether* they do?

## What I did

Survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier + Cox proportional hazards) on ADNI longitudinal cohort data, stratified by APOE ε4 allele dose (0, 1, or 2 copies), tracking time-to-conversion from MCI to AD dementia. The Cox model estimates the hazard of conversion at time $t$ as a baseline hazard scaled by covariate effects:

$$h(t \mid X) = h_0(t) \exp(\beta_1 X_1 + \beta_2 X_2 + \cdots + \beta_p X_p)$$

with ε4 allele dose entered as one of the $X_i$ covariates.

## Result

A clear dose-response relationship: each additional ε4 allele copy is associated with a shorter time to conversion.

## What it means

APOE genotype is a practical, already-available stratification variable for enrichment in AD prevention trials — patients with more ε4 copies convert faster and could be prioritized for earlier intervention.
